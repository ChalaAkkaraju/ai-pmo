# Stakeholder Register and Engagement Plan — Riverside Water Reclamation Phase 2 Expansion

> **Note on this document's role as a worked example.** This is the COMMITTED stakeholder register for Riverside Water, as it stood at end of the Planning phase (six weeks after charter approval), and as it evolved through the project. When the Stakeholder Analyst agent uses this as a worked example for analysing a new project's stakeholders, the items shown with italic inline annotations — influence ratings, engagement frequency, and any named individuals beyond those committed in the charter — should appear at the draft stage as `[NEEDS PM REVIEW: <value>]` rather than as the resolved values shown here. The final values shown are what the Northwood PM confirmed during the planning sequence; a new project's draft register would carry these in flagged form until equivalent confirmation cycles complete.

## 4. Engagement plan summary

Three messages run through the whole engagement plan, calibrated by audience:

**To the client side (D. Hoffmann, K. Velasquez, E. Marsh, City Council):** the message is *delivery confidence with disciplined honesty about risk*. Monthly written status to D. Hoffmann; weekly informal voice; biweekly site walks; quarterly direct touch with the Public Works Director; council briefings prepared in advance of any council session that may discuss the project. The Plant Superintendent gets a separate operational track focused on shutdown coordination and operator training.

**To regulators (State Environmental Agency, EPA):** the message is *we know the rules and we meet them*. Monthly compliance call with the assigned state engineer once named; formal submissions on the permit-driven schedule; no proactive EPA engagement.

**To the community and press (neighbourhood association, downstream users, local press):** the message is *transparency with calm framing*. Engagement is led by the City's PIO with Northwood support; quarterly community newsletter; complaint-response procedure with a 48-hour turnaround commitment; press protocol routes all enquiries through City first, with Northwood providing factual background only on request. *(at draft stage the press protocol section was `[NEEDS PM REVIEW: confirm whether Northwood comments directly or routes to City]`; the routed-via-City protocol was agreed at kickoff and held through the project.)*

## 5. How this register evolved (retrospective notes — not part of the worked-example pattern)

For context only — these are observations the Riverside PM recorded at closeout, not behaviours the Stakeholder Analyst agent should imitate when drafting:

- The blower vendor account lead (item 11) initially rated Medium influence in the draft register but was promoted to High at Week -12 when delivery slipped from 28 to 42 weeks; the schedule-impact made them the highest-impact external stakeholder for a window of about three months.
- The downstream river users (item 14) never required active engagement — the prediction held.
- The press (item 15) flipped briefly to High Interest at Week 18 when a local environmental group filed a complaint about night-time construction noise; the pre-agreed City-routed protocol handled it without escalation.

The register was reviewed and updated formally at each gate review (proposal-to-charter, planning, mobilisation, mid-construction, pre-test, closeout). Roughly six refresh cycles across the 22-month project.

## 6. Notes on the format (for the agent reading this as a worked example)

Three patterns the Stakeholder Analyst should imitate when drafting a register for a new project:

1. **Named individuals where committed; flagged placeholders where not.** Where the charter or intake names someone, use the name. Where it does not, use a generic role descriptor with an inline italic annotation showing the draft-stage `[NEEDS PM REVIEW: <what to confirm>]` form and (if applicable) how it was later resolved. Per-line discipline; do not apply a section-wide format.

2. **Influence and interest ratings each carry rationale.** A High / Medium / Low rating without rationale is unauditable. Even one-clause rationale ("political accountability to council") is enough. The matrix in §3 inherits the ratings from §2; do not re-justify there.

3. **The engagement plan in §4 follows from the matrix in §3, which follows from the register in §2.** If a stakeholder appears in §4 with a different strategy than §3 implies, that is a drafting error. Internal consistency across §2–§4 is the discipline.
