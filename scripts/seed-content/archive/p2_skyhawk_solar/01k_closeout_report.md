# Project Closeout Report — Skyhawk Solar + Storage Phase 1

> **Note on this document's role as a worked example.** This is the formal Project Closeout Report for Skyhawk Solar at Substantial Completion (5 June year+1), prepared and issued to the client, the Northwood Sponsor, and the portfolio dashboard. The report is the project's headline outcome document — the artefact a client signs against, the Sponsor cites at portfolio review, and Operations references during warranty. When the Closeout Reporter agent uses this as a worked example for a new project, two adaptations apply. First, at a Week 0 baseline state, the agent produces the closeout-report *framework* — the section structure, the outcome dimensions to be measured against charter §3 objectives, the audience-disclosure conventions, the risk-closeout-status discipline, the cross-reference points with the lessons-learned synthesis — not an outcome report on events that have not yet occurred. Second, items shown with inline italic annotations — specific numbers settled at SC, named events that occurred during execution, lessons-learned headlines drawn from the synthesis — would carry `[NEEDS PM REVIEW]` flags at draft stage. The closeout state shown here reflects what the Skyhawk PM and Project Director committed at Substantial Completion.

## 1. Executive summary

**Headline outcome.** Skyhawk Solar + Storage Phase 1 achieved Substantial Completion on schedule (5 June year+1, original contractual SC date) with all charter §3 objectives met or substantially met. Closing margin: **8.6%** against bid margin 8.8% — within 0.2 percentage points of target and well above the 5.1% commercially-distressed floor. Zero lost-time injuries across 14 months of construction and 215,000 work-hours. Performance test passed first attempt against both nameplate (200 MW DC PV array) and storage (100 MWh / 4-hour BESS) targets. Grid interconnection energisation achieved 1 May year+1 per charter Objective 3, supporting the client's commissioning window.

**Commercial outcome.** Contract value increased through three approved change orders from $68.0M to $69.85M (+$1.85M, +2.7%). Final cost $63.85M against approved budget $62.0M and revised approved budget $63.7M (post-CO). Contingency consumption $2.1M of $2.5M envelope (84%). Margin recovery in the final three months brought closing margin from a 7.3% mid-execution projection (Week 28 variance report) to 8.6% at SC through favourable module-cost variance and disciplined punch-list closeout.

**Client relationship.** Client relationship intact and strengthened. Skyhawk Renewables LLC has indicated Phase 2 prospect (additional 100 MW PV + 50 MWh BESS at a separate site) for award decision in Q3 next fiscal year; Northwood positioned as preferred bidder. Final client survey rated Northwood at 8.7/10 across the four standard dimensions (delivery, commercial, technical, safety).

**Lessons captured.** Seven firm-level lessons documented in the companion Lessons Learned Synthesis (`01_lessons_learned_skyhawk.md` — referenced; not part of this report). Headline lessons quoted in §8.

## 8. Lessons learned — headlines from companion synthesis

The full Lessons Learned Synthesis (companion document) contains seven firm-level recommendations and three project-specific lessons. The five headline lessons quoted here are the ones expected to influence Northwood standard practice and follow-on renewables-plus-storage projects.

1. **Vendor financial-health monitoring on sole-source long-lead procurement should be monthly, not quarterly, during the PO-release window.** Evidence: BESS vendor financial deterioration was detected at Q3 monitoring window in time to reselect at Week 18; a monthly cadence would have surfaced indicators 4–8 weeks earlier and potentially avoided the $400k premium.

2. **Geotechnical investigation grid density on solar projects with variable soil profiles should be uniform across the footprint, not topographically-prioritised.** Evidence: Week 22-24 civil sub-grade variance in an unsampled north-west quadrant of Plot B. Updated Northwood standard for variable-terrain solar projects.

3. **Pre-monsoon work-front loading discipline is operationally validated as a mitigation for tropical-climate solar construction.** Evidence: The pre-monsoon erosion-control completion by Week 34 (per the Week 28 variance recovery action) absorbed the monsoon productivity loss within the lower end of the projected range. The discipline should be a standard mitigation in tropical-climate renewables proposals.

4. **T&M variation mechanism for BESS vendor commercial events worked as designed.** Evidence: CO-002 ($400k Northwood-driven absorption) was processed in 9 days from vendor financial-health flag to client co-signature. The variation language from the Skyhawk contract is now Northwood standard for BESS procurement.

5. **Client-relationship value can legitimately justify margin compression on mid-construction client-driven scope additions, but the rationale must be explicit and pre-approved.** Evidence: CO-003 ($1.85M client-driven SCADA integration) was accepted at 7.0% margin against bid 8.8% with Phase 2 prospect cited in the four-frame pricing rationale. The Phase 2 prospect has subsequently materialised as a Q3-next-fiscal-year award decision; the strategic value is being realised. The discipline of making the strategic input explicit at change-order analysis (not implicit) is the lesson.

## 10. Conventions used

Five patterns this document follows, for traceability against the Northwood worked-example convention:

1. **Objective-by-objective binary status with quantitative supporting metrics.** Charter §3 objectives are the report's spine; each gets a Met / Partially met / Not met status plus the underlying numbers. A closeout report that buries the objective performance in narrative fails its primary audience (client and Sponsor). The binary status format is the discipline that surfaces outcome at headline level.

2. **Risk register closeout status with three categories.** Realised / mitigated / not materialised, with cited evidence (contingency consumption, variance section, change-order ID). The three-category framing prevents the "all risks managed successfully" closeout summary that obscures which risks actually appeared and which did not.

3. **Audience-aware disclosure discipline.** The Executive Summary is the client-and-Sponsor-facing headline; the Financial Summary (§3) is full-detail for the Sponsor and portfolio dashboard (no branch-level detail to client beyond what is contractually required); the Lessons Learned section (§8) quotes only headlines from the synthesis (full synthesis is internal-only unless client-facing lessons are pre-approved).

4. **Lessons-learned cross-reference, not lessons-learned duplication.** The closeout report quotes headline lessons (§8); the companion Lessons Learned Synthesis carries the full synthesis with evidence and firm-level recommendations. Closeout reports that try to be the lessons-learned document fail at both jobs.

5. **Follow-on and handover items separated from outcome items.** §9 collects operational handover, client-relationship continuation, portfolio transfer, and documentation archive items — the forward-looking dimensions distinct from the outcome reporting in §1–§8. The separation makes the operational handover actionable.
