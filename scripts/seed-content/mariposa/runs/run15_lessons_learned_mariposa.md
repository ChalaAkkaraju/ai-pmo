## 2. Executive summary

Mariposa is at Week 0 baseline. No execution has occurred, no lessons are yet realised, and no firm-level adoptions can yet be committed. The executive summary at Week 0 identifies the **headline lessons-to-track** — the five most likely transferable-learning outcomes if their precursor issues materialise — in priority order for prospective firm-level adoption.

The five headline lessons-to-track, in priority order:

1. **Single-OEM concentration across 80 turbines is a category-level test of the Ironvale firm-level standard.** Northwood adopted the single-sourced-critical-equipment policy update Q3 post-Ironvale-closeout (monthly OEM financial-health monitoring, sole-source risk classification). Mariposa is the first major renewables project executing under that updated standard, and at 80-unit concentration it tests the standard at a scale Ironvale did not. If the OEM PO and FAT discipline (WBS 3.1, 3.11) executes cleanly, Mariposa validates the firm-level standard at renewables scale; if it does not, the standard needs renewables-specific calibration. [Firm-level candidate — validates or refines existing standard]

2. **Tax-credit-deadline-driven projects warrant pre-contract confirmation discipline that Mariposa's Week 0 state shows is currently missing.** Five H-severity issues at Week 0 (I-001 Client Lead, I-002 OEM PO release, I-003 blackout windows, I-004 camp decision, I-005 interconnection study) are all items that ideally would have closed before contract signature on a project where the energisation date is the central commercial commitment. If Mariposa executes successfully despite the open items, the lesson is that Northwood's execution discipline can absorb late-resolution items; if any of the items drives material variance, the lesson is that bid-stage diligence on tax-credit projects needs to extend to these confirmations as contract preconditions. [Firm-level candidate]

3. **The 30-day SC-to-energisation buffer is either sufficient or insufficient on tax-credit-deadline projects; Mariposa will produce the evidence.** The buffer is the single most-watched figure on the project per the variance framework and communications plan. If the buffer absorbs the cumulative slip from R1–R5 realisations without erosion past the 30 December year+1 hard deadline, 30 days is the validated minimum; if it erodes, the lesson is that future tax-credit projects need a longer buffer baselined into the schedule. [Firm-level candidate]

4. **Camp accommodation build-vs-lease decision timing affects mobilisation and budget structure materially.** Mariposa's Week 0 state carries this decision as unresolved (I-004); the outcome of when and how it resolves will produce a transferable lesson on whether rural-location renewables bids should price the camp decision before contract signature where labour-market intelligence permits. [Firm-level candidate]

5. **Two-county DOT coordination on rural-road upgrades is a parallel critical-path risk class that Mariposa is the first renewables-scale test of within Northwood's portfolio.** Charter §8 R4 and WBS branch 4.0 commit this as a Day-1 mitigation focus. Lessons on whether engagement-from-Day-1, dedicated Logistics Lead, and parallel-rather-than-serial work where DOTs permit are sufficient mitigation strategies — or whether the strategies need additional components — will land at the close of Chain C. [Firm-level candidate]

These five headline lessons-to-track drive the §4 prospective firm-level recommendations. The §3 synthesis identifies a fuller theme-by-theme list of lessons-to-track. The §5 project-specific lessons section is empty at Week 0 — project-specific lessons can only be identified retrospectively once execution circumstances are visible. §6 follow-on project recommendations contain only the dimensions visible at Week 0 (commercial-position and client-relationship dimensions); operational-template recommendations require execution evidence.

## 3. Synthesis by theme — lessons-to-track at Week 0

The lessons-to-track below are organised by six themes that recur across the Week 0 issue log, the charter §8 risk register, and Mariposa's structural characteristics. Each lesson-to-track is anchored to its precursor issue or risk, carries a pre-classification flag, and identifies the closure event or variance event that would generate the realised lesson.

### 3.1 Procurement and vendor management

**Single-OEM concentration at 80 units tests the post-Ironvale firm-level standard at renewables scale.** Precursor: I-002 (OEM PO release date pending), I-007 (OEM organisation and account lead unnamed), charter §8 R1, WBS 3.1 ($67.0M, 50% of cost base per budget §3). The Ironvale firm-level standard (Operations Standards, adopted Q3 post-Ironvale-closeout) prescribes monthly financial-health monitoring and sole-source risk classification for single-OEM critical equipment. Mariposa's 80-unit concentration is a quantitative step beyond Ironvale's two-furnace concentration. Realised lesson lands when OEM PO is released and FAT discipline on lead units (WBS 3.11) executes; expected timing Weeks 4–12. If FAT discipline is sufficient at this scale, Mariposa validates the standard for renewables; if FAT discipline reveals scale-specific gaps (e.g., monthly cadence too infrequent for 80-unit manufacturing run), the standard needs renewables-specific calibration. [Firm-level candidate]

**FAT on lead units mandatory above $5M is being executed against the Ironvale post-closeout standard.** Precursor: WBS 3.11, charter §8 R1 mitigation. The Ironvale-derived Q1-post-closeout standard prescribes FAT on lead units above $5M. Mariposa's $67M turbine OEM PO triggers this standard. Realised lesson lands at FAT completion on lead turbine units; if FAT surfaces issues that propagate to fleet (the Ironvale refractory-geometry pattern), the lesson confirms the Q1 standard's value at renewables scale. [Firm-level candidate — validates existing standard]

**OEM expediter discipline on the ground at OEM factory tests the operational mechanism of the Ironvale post-closeout monitoring standard.** Precursor: stakeholder register row 8, communications plan §3.4. Communications plan §3.4 commits to an on-site expediter at OEM factory; the start window relative to first delivery is flagged for PM review. Realised lesson on whether on-the-ground expediter cadence and start window are the right operational mechanism at 80-unit scale lands during the manufacturing phase. [Firm-level candidate]

### 3.2 Engineering and design — schedule-discipline and interface-coordination

**60% Engineering gate as PO-release precondition tests the post-Ironvale OEM PO-readiness discipline.** Precursor: I-002, I-013 (charter §6 milestone dates flagged), WBS 2.11 (OEM design interface), schedule Chain A. The Ironvale Week 0 lesson surfaced that single-OEM concentration risks require PO-readiness tracking from contract signature; Mariposa's schedule Chain A → B sequence operationalises this. If the 60% Engineering gate is sufficient PO-readiness precondition (no rework-driven OEM PO amendments post-release), Mariposa validates the discipline; if not, the standard needs gate-level refinement. [Firm-level candidate]

**OEM design-interface coordination (WBS 2.11) is the renewables-scale equivalent of the Ironvale embedded-engineer arrangement.** Precursor: WBS 2.11, charter §8 R1. The Ironvale embedded-client-engineer arrangement (T. Hartnett) eliminated coordination friction between Engineering 60% and Mechanical Completion. Mariposa does not have an analogous embedded arrangement at Week 0; the OEM design interface package is the structural substitute. Realised lesson on whether structured-package coordination achieves comparable friction reduction lands during detailed design (Weeks 4–30). [Firm-level candidate — refines Ironvale standard for OEM-side rather than client-side]

### 3.3 Construction execution — regulatory, weather, and labour

**Migratory bird flyway blackout-window confirmation as a pre-mobilisation hard deliverable.** Precursor: I-003, charter §8 R3, charter §10 Constraint 3, WBS 5.2 sequencing constraint. Charter §9 Assumption 3 carries the blackout windows into Week 0 as pending client confirmation. Realised lesson lands when client confirms windows; expected timing Weeks 3–8. If confirmed windows match bid assumptions, the lesson is that proposal-stage diligence on protected-species flyways is sufficient; if confirmed windows differ materially, the lesson is that future projects in protected-species flyways need pre-contract written confirmation of blackout dates as a hard deliverable. [Firm-level candidate]

**Two-county DOT coordination on rural-road upgrades as parallel critical-path discipline.** Precursor: I-006, charter §8 R4, WBS branch 4.0, schedule Chain C. Mitigation strategy committed in charter §8 R4 (Day-1 engagement, dedicated Logistics Lead, parallel work where DOTs permit). Realised lesson lands at completion of Chain C (both counties' upgrades complete); if the strategy is sufficient, the lesson validates the approach for renewables projects with cross-jurisdictional rural-road dependency; if either county becomes the binding constraint despite the mitigation, the lesson identifies which strategy components need strengthening. [Firm-level candidate]

**Camp accommodation build-vs-lease decision timing as a pre-mobilisation deliverable.** Precursor: I-004, charter §8 R5, charter §9 Assumption 6, WBS 1.8 and 3.12, budget $1.7M Medium-confidence line. Realised lesson lands at mobilisation (expected Weeks 6–10); if decision is timely and the chosen arrangement matches labour-market reality, the lesson is that the decision timing was appropriate; if decision is late or wrong-fit, the lesson is that rural-location renewables bids need camp pricing before contract signature. [Firm-level candidate]

**Geotechnical investigation at 80 turbine positions as a brownfield-vs-greenfield discipline test.** Precursor: WBS 2.1, 5.4. The Ironvale Q1 post-closeout standard committed uniform-density geotechnical investigation for industrial brownfield. Mariposa is greenfield, so the standard does not directly apply, but the question of whether 80-position investigation density needs adjustment from standard renewables practice will surface during foundations construction. [Project-specific candidate — greenfield circumstances reduce transferability to Ironvale's brownfield standard]

### 3.4 Commissioning and performance — energisation discipline

**The 30-day SC-to-energisation buffer as a sufficient-or-insufficient test on tax-credit-deadline projects.** Precursor: charter §10 Constraint 1, schedule §3 Chain F, variance §2 buffer-watch metric. The buffer is the project's only float against the hard 30 December year+1 federal tax-credit deadline. Realised lesson lands at energisation (expected on or before 30 December year+1); if cumulative variance from R1–R5 realisations does not erode the buffer past acceptability, the lesson is that 30 days is the validated minimum on tax-credit projects; if the buffer erodes, the lesson is that future tax-credit projects need a longer buffer baselined into the schedule. [Firm-level candidate]

**Performance test procedure agreement window with utility and permitting authority.** Precursor: I-019, WBS 9.5 and 9.6, communications plan §3.2 pre-test briefing. Realised lesson on whether the procedure-agreement window (pre-test) is sufficient for tax-credit-deadline projects lands at performance test execution; if window is too tight and procedure ambiguity propagates to test rerun, the lesson refines pre-test discipline. [Firm-level candidate]

### 3.5 Commercial and contract structure

**Fixed-price contract structure on a project with material engineering-discovery risk is a structural-fit test.** Precursor: charter §1 contract type, budget §2 commercial structure. Ironvale closeout established T&M-with-cap as the commercial structure suited to engineering-discovery-sensitive scope and adopted T&M-with-cap as a standard commercial option Q2 post-Ironvale-closeout. Mariposa is fixed-price. Lessons on whether fixed-price is the right structure for a renewables greenfield with single-OEM concentration and tax-credit deadline land at project close; the comparison against Ironvale's T&M-with-cap commercial outcome (12.3% closing margin) and Mariposa's fixed-price commercial outcome will identify whether renewables-greenfield should remain fixed-price or whether the T&M-with-cap option (Q2 post-Ironvale firm-level standard) should be offered for renewables work too. [Firm-level candidate]

**Change-order approval thresholds and pre-agreed unit rates as a Week 0 governance gap.** Precursor: I-014. Charter §11 flags change-order thresholds for confirmation. Realised lesson on whether the gap closure timing (target Week 1–4) is fast enough to prevent change-order processing-delay during execution lands at the first change order received. [Project-specific candidate — governance items are typically project-specific corrections rather than firm-level patterns]

**Contract risk-allocation language on regulatory-shift events as a commercial-recovery-channel test.** Precursor: change-order framework §10 forward-flag. Mariposa's contract risk-allocation on regulatory-shift events is unconfirmed at Week 0. Realised lesson on whether the contract's risk allocation supports commercial recovery from wildlife-agency, utility, or county-DOT-driven changes lands at the first such change order; if recovery channel is constrained, the lesson identifies the contract clause that future renewables proposals should strengthen. [Firm-level candidate]

### 3.6 Stakeholder and communications

**Client Lead confirmation as a pre-execution prerequisite on tax-credit-deadline projects.** Precursor: I-001, stakeholder register row 6, communications plan §3.1. Charter §5 carries Client Lead as unnamed at Week 0. Realised lesson on whether the absence of a confirmed Client Lead at Week 0 degrades escalation pathways during early execution lands when the lead is named and engagement preferences are documented; expected timing Weeks 1–2. If naming is timely and engagement is responsive, the lesson is that Week 0 absence is recoverable; if not, the lesson is that client-lead confirmation should be a contract-execution precondition on tax-credit-driven projects. [Firm-level candidate]

**Client Executive Sponsor distinction from Client Lead on IPP-class clients.** Precursor: I-017, stakeholder register row 7. IPP-class clients (independent power producers) typically have a separate Client Executive Sponsor responsible for tax-credit outcome and financing covenant alignment, distinct from the Client Lead responsible for day-to-day commercial interface. Mariposa's organisation chart at Week 0 does not confirm this. Realised lesson on the IPP-class stakeholder pattern lands when the client organisation is mapped; if Mariposa Renewables Holdings does have a distinct executive sponsor, the lesson validates the IPP-class stakeholder-register convention; if not, the convention may need IPP-subcategory refinement. [Firm-level candidate]

**Community-engagement responsibility split with client on renewables projects.** Precursor: I-018, stakeholder register row 15. The communications plan commits a client-led-with-Northwood-support model. Realised lesson on whether the split is operationally workable lands during construction; the contingent risk is the landowner-opposition flip scenario in stakeholder register row 15 and crisis-class §6.4. [Firm-level candidate]

## 7. Conventions used

Five patterns this document follows, for traceability against the Northwood worked-example convention (Ironvale closeout lessons-learned synthesis):

1. **Synthesis is theme-led, not chronology-led.** Lessons-to-track organised by six themes (Procurement and vendor management; Engineering and design; Construction execution; Commissioning and performance; Commercial and contract structure; Stakeholder and communications), not by week or by issue ID. Theme-led synthesis extracts the signal even at Week 0 framework state.

2. **Every lesson-to-track has cited precursor evidence from upstream artefacts.** Issue log entries (I-NNN), charter §8 risks (R1–R5), WBS packages, schedule chains, budget branches are the precursor base. Lessons-to-track without cited precursor evidence are inadmissible — they may be plausible but they are unsupported speculation. The same evidence discipline that admits realised lessons admits lessons-to-track.

3. **Recommendations classified by adoption pathway and owner function.** Five pathways (Operations Standards, Commercial / Proposal Standards, Project Methodology Standards, Engineering Standards, Contract Standards) with five corresponding owner functions. At Week 0 the status field is "Pending — realises at [event]" rather than the closeout "Adopted Qn post-closeout"; the discipline is the same, only the state differs.

4. **Firm-level vs project-specific separation explicit and pre-classified at Week 0.** §4 contains prospective firm-level recommendations; §5 is empty at Week 0 because project-specific lessons require execution circumstances. Each Week 0 lesson-to-track in §3 carries a pre-classification flag — [Firm-level candidate] or [Project-specific candidate] — so the PM monitoring the project knows where transferable knowledge is most likely to surface.

5. **Adoption status tracked at the lesson level, with explicit Week-0-prospective hedging.** Each prospective firm-level recommendation in §4 has a "Pending — realises at [event]" status field. This is the Week 0 equivalent of the closeout "Adopted Qn post-closeout" status. The lessons-learned document is a *living artefact* updated through the project lifecycle; the Week 0 baseline commits the framework against which closeout populates the realised lessons.
