## 4. Engagement plan summary

Three messages run through the engagement plan, calibrated by audience.

**To the client side (Client Lead, Client Executive Sponsor):** the message is *energisation by 30 December year+1 is the organising commitment, and we will tell you early and honestly about anything that threatens it*. Monthly written status to the Client Lead; weekly informal voice contact; biweekly site walks once mobilised; quarterly direct touch with the Client Executive Sponsor; immediate escalation on any event that could move the energisation date. The tax-credit deadline is the single point of alignment; every status conversation references the energisation critical path.

**To regulators (Permitting Authority, Utility Coordinator):** the message is *we know the rules and we meet them*. Monthly compliance touch with the assigned wildlife agency officer once named; formal permit submissions on schedule; monthly utility coordination call from contract effective date through energisation; no surprises on blackout-period adherence or interconnection milestones. Permit-driven dates are treated as fixed; the schedule is built around them, not the other way round.

**To the community and press (local landowner group, regional press, county councils):** the message is *transparency with calm framing, led by the client*. Community engagement is client-led with Northwood support; quarterly community newsletter via client communications; complaint-response procedure with a 48-hour turnaround. Press enquiries route through the client communications lead with Northwood providing factual background only on request. County councils are briefed via the County Roads Leads ahead of any council session where the project may be discussed. *(at draft stage the press protocol and community-engagement split are [NEEDS PM REVIEW: confirm with client at kickoff].)*

**To internal Northwood (Sponsor, Project Director, Construction Manager, Logistics Lead, subcontractor leads):** the message is *one schedule, one critical path, and the OEM and the road upgrades are where the project is won or lost*. Weekly internal status; daily on the critical path during construction; monthly portfolio dashboard to the Sponsor; escalation per the charter governance ladder (PM → Director → Sponsor → CFO). The single-OEM concentration risk and the two-county road coordination risk are standing agenda items at every internal review until both are de-risked.
