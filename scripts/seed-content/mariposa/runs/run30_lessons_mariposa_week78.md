## 2. Executive summary

Mariposa closed at SC at 9.5% margin (at bid target — recovered from Week 52's 9.3% compression) on a project that absorbed one structurally-predicted client-driven change order, one contained site-conditions event, and one weather event without eroding the 30-day SC-to-energisation buffer or breaching the 5.7% margin floor. Five lessons account for the outperformance pattern and are the recommended Northwood standard-practice updates; a sixth is provisional pending warranty-tail evidence.

**Five headline lessons, in priority order for Northwood firm-level adoption:**

1. **The four-frame commercial dynamics discipline preserves margin on client-driven mid-construction scope additions on fixed-price contracts.** Mariposa CO-001 closed at 8.2% realised margin via Vendor leverage / Client leverage / Client position / Northwood acceptance analysis; Riverside CO-003 (pre-discipline) closed at 6.7% on a structurally similar event. 1.5 percentage-point margin protection on the closed CO event is the first portfolio-validated renewables-scale empirical anchor for the post-Ironvale Commercial Standards. The discipline is now portfolio-tested across two segments (water, renewables) plus the Ironvale industrial origin. [Firm-level adoption]

2. **For variable-terrain greenfield wind sites, geotechnical investigation grid density at proposal stage should exceed standard greenfield grid.** Mariposa I-021 / R-009 produced a three-position localised-and-contained sub-grade variance arc at Plot B (positions 16, 23, 41) empirically identical to Skyhawk Plot B NW quadrant — two-of-two greenfield realisations following the same containment pattern. The Ironvale Q1-post-closeout standard (uniform-density grid for industrial brownfield) needs a parallel greenfield-wind variant. Riverside (water-treatment brownfield) remains the structural exception that confirms the segment-specificity of the lesson. [Firm-level adoption]

3. **The 30-day SC-to-energisation buffer is structurally sufficient against mid-construction shocks on tax-credit-deadline projects.** The buffer survived three material in-construction events (I-021 sub-grade Week 27, I-028 weather Week 49, Chain E float consumption Week 52) without erosion; SC achieved on contractual date; energisation forecast within deadline. The Week 0 lessons-to-track headline (#3) resolves in the affirmative: 30 days is the validated minimum, not a longer baseline. [Firm-level adoption]

4. **Single-OEM concentration risk on fleet projects above 50 units is testable through layered discipline: monthly financial-health monitoring + FAT-on-lead-units + on-site expediter + lead-unit-erection-as-precedent + fleet performance-test contingency window.** Mariposa's full execution arc (I-002 Week 18 → I-024 FAT Week 33 → I-027 first erection Week 47 → I-029 fleet performance-test Week 75) is the most complete renewables-scale validation of the post-Ironvale discipline to date. The I-029 finding refines the standard by extending "expect findings, time-budget them" from manufacturing acceptance (FAT) to operational acceptance (168-hour availability test): a 7–10 working day performance-test contingency window is recommended for fleet projects above 50 units. [Firm-level adoption]

5. **For tax-credit-tier-verification dispatch integration on IPP projects, proposal-stage scoping should explicitly clarify whether SCADA scope ends at plant control or extends to client portfolio OPCO.** I-026 / CO-001 — had the OPCO boundary been clarified at proposal stage, the integration would have priced into base contract rather than as a mid-construction CO, eliminating the 1.3 percentage-point Week 52 margin compression entirely. The lesson refines proposal-stage scope-statement discipline on IPP-class clients with portfolio operations centres. [Firm-level adoption]

A sixth lesson is provisional: **R5-pooled weather contingency sized against erection-season-climate-band expected stand-down windows is operationally robust** — I-028 4-day stand-down absorbed cleanly, remaining erection season clean, R5 90% intact at SC after sub-grade and weather reallocations. The lesson is confirmed for Mariposa but elevates to firm-level only once a second renewables-segment project validates the sizing approach. [Firm-level candidate — confirmed-on-Mariposa, pending second-project validation]

These five-plus-one headline lessons drive the §4 firm-level recommendations. The §3 synthesis carries the full theme-by-theme set; the §5 project-specific lessons capture additional events of narrower applicability.

## 3. Synthesis by theme

The lessons are organised by six themes that recur across the issue log, variance analyses, change-order analysis, and risk register. Each theme contains specific lessons with evidence cited from the source artefacts. The Week 0 framework's six-theme structure carries forward; lessons-to-track from the Week 0 baseline are resolved here against execution evidence.

### 3.1 Procurement and vendor management

Three lessons from procurement and OEM management.

**Single-OEM concentration at 80 units validated the post-Ironvale firm-level standard at renewables scale and refined it for operational acceptance.** Precursor: Week 0 lessons-to-track §3.1 headline. Evidence: I-002 PO release Week 18 at $66.4M against $67.0M budget (favourable $0.6M); I-007 OEM account lead confirmed; I-024 FAT clear Week 33 with rotor-hub torque procedure refinement and blade-pitch calibration drift remediated; I-027 first-turbine erection Week 47 validated procedure; all 80 deliveries Week 50; all 80 erections Week 70; I-029 performance-test re-calibration on 2 of 80 (positions 47, 62) closed under OEM warranty Week 75 at zero Northwood cost. The Ironvale Q3-post-closeout single-sourced-critical-equipment standard (monthly financial-health monitoring, sole-source risk classification, on-site expediter) executed cleanly at renewables scale. **The I-029 finding refines the standard:** wear-in calibration drift detected during the 168-hour fleet availability test is a distinct mode from FAT manufacturing-drift, is characteristic of operational acceptance, and warrants a 7–10 working day performance-test contingency window at fleet-level availability test stage on projects above 50 units. Recommend Northwood elevate the standard with this refinement.

**FAT-on-lead-units-above-$5M standard (Ironvale Q1 post-closeout) validated at $67M renewables OEM PO.** Precursor: WBS 3.11. Evidence: I-024 FAT findings remediated Week 33; refined torque procedure adopted fleet-wide; one punch-list item carried to first-turbine erection (I-027); no fleet-wide propagation. The Ironvale-derived standard held at the largest single OEM PO Northwood has executed under it. Confirms the standard at renewables scale.

**On-site expediter cadence at OEM factory worked operationally.** Precursor: communications plan §3.4 commitment. Evidence: expediter deployed Week 22 (4 weeks after PO release); demobilised Week 50 at final delivery. No deliveries slipped contracted cadence. Validates the on-the-ground operational mechanism at 80-unit scale.

### 3.2 Engineering and design

Two lessons from engineering execution.

**The OEM design-interface package functioned as a renewables-scale substitute for the Ironvale embedded-engineer arrangement.** Precursor: WBS 2.11; Week 0 lessons-to-track §3.2. Evidence: formal design-interface review Week 19; I-024 FAT findings remediated within OEM warranty channel; I-027 first-turbine erection validated procedure; 79 subsequent erections without further design-interface findings. The structural alternative to embedded-engineer coordination works on the OEM-supplier side of the relationship; Ironvale's lesson was client-side embedment, Mariposa's is OEM-side structured package — distinct mechanisms, comparable friction reduction. Refines the Ironvale standard for OEM-side coordination on first-of-its-kind fleet projects.

**Pre-acceptance-test data-tag reconciliation gate on SCADA portfolio integration scope.** Precursor: I-030, CO-001 amendment condition 3. Evidence: CO-001 OPCO acceptance test first attempt Week 65 revealed 12-of-1,400 data-tag mismatches; jointly remediated within one week; <$30k absorbed within CO-001 budget tolerance. The data-model snapshot mechanism (CO-001 condition 3) protected commercial position correctly; the methodological lesson is preventive — a pre-test reconciliation gate 1–2 weeks before formal acceptance test is a low-cost methodological improvement that would eliminate the rework cycle. Secondary lesson under Pattern 3 firm-level elevation.

### 3.3 Construction execution

Three lessons from on-site construction.

**Variable-terrain greenfield wind sites need geotechnical investigation grid density exceeding standard greenfield grid at proposal stage.** Precursor: Week 0 lessons-to-track §3.3 (Project-specific candidate at Week 0 — re-classified to Firm-level at closeout per cross-portfolio evidence). Evidence: I-021 sub-grade variance at Plot B positions 16, 23, 41 realised Week 27; remediated Week 30; extended geotechnical investigation Weeks 29-31 confirmed no further variance; commissioning-phase settlement monitoring Weeks 65-78 showed no anomaly at remediated positions; $0.18M absorbed within R5 reserve. The pattern is empirically identical to Skyhawk Plot B NW quadrant — two-of-two greenfield wind realisations follow the same localised-and-contained arc. The Ironvale Q1-post-closeout uniform-density standard for industrial brownfield needs a parallel greenfield-wind variant. **Re-classification rationale:** at Week 0 the lesson was flagged Project-specific because greenfield is a different category from Ironvale's brownfield; at closeout, the cross-portfolio Skyhawk evidence elevates it to Firm-level for the greenfield-wind segment specifically.

**R5-pooled weather contingency sized against erection-season-climate-band expected stand-down windows.** Precursor: charter §8 R-010; I-028. Evidence: Week 49 4-day weather stand-down (I-028) absorbed within Chain E float and R5/unallocated reserve at $0.20M; remaining erection season Weeks 50-70 saw no further multi-day stand-down events; R5 reserve 90% intact at SC after sub-grade and weather reallocations. The pooled-reserve approach (vs dedicated R-009 / R-010 allocations) preserved PM allocation discretion and absorbed the realised events cleanly. Confirms the sizing approach for renewables-greenfield erection seasons; elevates to firm-level provisional pending second-project validation.

**Demobilisation discipline on rural-camp and rural-road restoration.** Precursor: WBS 10.6, county DOT agreements. Evidence: camp wind-down and rural-road restoration handback in progress through Week 82 per WBS 10.6 and county DOT agreements; no demobilisation-phase incidents through Week 78. Operational discipline; not transferable as a firm-level lesson but worth recording.

### 3.4 Commissioning and performance

Two lessons from commissioning and performance test.

**Fleet-level performance testing on single-OEM projects above 50 units should budget for ~2-3% first-pass failure on the 168-hour availability test with second-attempt resolution under OEM warranty as the standard pattern.** Precursor: I-029. Evidence: 78 of 80 turbines passed first attempt (97.5%); 2 of 80 (positions 47, 62) re-calibrated under OEM warranty and passed second attempt within OEM PO warranty obligations; zero Northwood cost; performance bond mechanism not invoked. The first-pass rate is within OEM-stated fleet-level expectation. The lesson extends the Ironvale FAT-discipline lesson — "expect findings, time-budget them" — from manufacturing acceptance to operational acceptance. Recommend explicit performance-test contingency window of 7-10 working days at fleet-level availability test stage on projects above 50 units.

**The 30-day SC-to-energisation buffer is structurally sufficient against mid-construction shocks on tax-credit-deadline projects.** Precursor: Week 0 lessons-to-track §3.4 headline. Evidence: buffer survived three material in-construction events (I-021 Week 27, I-028 Week 49, Chain E float consumption Week 52) without erosion; SC achieved on contractual date; energisation forecast Week 82 within tax-credit deadline. The Week 0 lessons-to-track headline resolves in the affirmative — 30 days is the validated minimum, not a longer baseline required.

### 3.5 Commercial and contract structure

Two lessons from commercial outcomes.

**The four-frame commercial dynamics discipline preserves margin on client-driven mid-construction scope additions on fixed-price contracts.** Precursor: Week 0 lessons-to-track §3.5 (T&M-with-cap structural-fit test). Evidence: CO-001 SCADA portfolio integration negotiated Weeks 34-38, executed Week 40, closed at 8.2% realised margin via four-frame analysis (Vendor leverage favourable / Client leverage low / Client position strong / Northwood acceptance — Phase 2 prospect named explicitly). Riverside CO-003 (pre-discipline) closed at 6.7% on structurally similar event. 1.5 percentage-point margin protection on the closed CO event. First portfolio-validated application of post-Ironvale Commercial Standards at renewables scale. **The Week 0 lessons-to-track question — "is fixed-price the right structure for renewables-greenfield with engineering-discovery risk, or should T&M-with-cap be offered?" — resolves with a nuanced answer:** fixed-price worked at Mariposa because the four-frame discipline prevented the Riverside-style margin erosion that drove the Ironvale T&M-with-cap recommendation. T&M-with-cap remains a valuable alternative option for engineering-discovery-sensitive scope, but the four-frame discipline is the structural prevention that allows fixed-price to remain viable on tax-credit-deadline renewables work.

**Proposal-stage SCADA scope-statement clarity on plant-control-vs-portfolio-OPCO boundary on IPP-class clients.** Precursor: I-026, I-030, CO-001. Evidence: had the OPCO boundary been clarified at proposal stage, the integration would have priced into base contract rather than as CO-001, eliminating the 1.3 percentage-point Week 52 margin compression entirely. IPP-class clients with portfolio operations centres typically need dispatch-verification integration where tax-credit-tier qualification mechanisms operate; the scope boundary is predictable in advance.

## 5. Project-specific lessons (not for firm-level adoption)

Four lessons are specific to Mariposa's circumstances and do not warrant firm-level adoption.

**T. Caldwell and R. Holstein's specific county-DOT relationships are not directly replicable.** The two-county DOT coordination on rural-road upgrades (charter §8 R4, I-006) worked exceptionally well in part because both DOT Roads Leads were responsive and operationally aligned. The lesson is the parallel-not-serial engagement discipline (firm-level via Pattern 2 sub-class); the specific personalities and county-pair dynamics are not transferable. Future projects in different counties will have different specific dynamics.

**The Plot B sub-grade variance at three specific positions is unlikely to recur in the same form.** The Skyhawk + Mariposa pattern is the localised-and-contained arc (firm-level); the specific positions, soil composition, and remediation method are project-specific. Future variable-terrain greenfield wind sites will have different specific defects; the discipline applies, the specific pattern does not.

**The 12-of-1,400 CO-001 data-tag mismatch is specific to the Mariposa SCADA-to-OPCO data-model boundary.** The lesson is the pre-acceptance-test reconciliation gate (firm-level methodological item in §4); the specific tags and remediation are project-specific. Different SCADA-OPCO integrations will have different specific mismatches.

**G. Castillo's specific Week 33 verbal indication of Phase 2 capital planning is project-specific commercial intelligence.** The lesson is the IPP-class Phase 2 prospect dimension (named explicitly in CO-001 four-frame analysis per post-Ironvale Commercial Standards); the specific Phase 2 prospect is a Mariposa-specific commercial input that should not be assumed transferable to other IPP-class clients without independent confirmation.

## 7. Conventions used

Five patterns this document follows, for traceability against the Northwood worked-example convention (Ironvale closeout) and the Mariposa Week 0 framework:

1. **Synthesis is theme-led, not chronology-led.** Lessons organised by six themes (Procurement and vendor management; Engineering and design; Construction execution; Commissioning and performance; Commercial and contract structure; Stakeholder and communications). Six themes match the Week 0 framework structure; the closeout synthesis populates the framework rather than restructuring it.

2. **Every lesson has cited evidence from upstream artefacts.** Issue log entries (I-001 through I-031), risk register status (R-001 through R-012 with three-category closeout disposition), variance section references, CO-001 four-frame analysis, charter §8 risk register. Where a Week 0 lessons-to-track item resolves at closeout, both the precursor evidence and the realised evidence are cited so the resolution arc is auditable.

3. **Recommendations classified by adoption pathway and owner function.** Five pathways (Operations Standards, Commercial / Proposal Standards, Project Methodology Standards, Engineering Standards, Commercial / Contract Standards) with five corresponding owner functions. At SC, adoption status is committed-prospective ("Target adoption Q1 post-closeout") rather than realised; adoption is the post-closeout Northwood standard-practice update cycle. The Ironvale convention of tracking adoption-status through the first year post-closeout applies and will be reflected in document updates at Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 post-closeout.

4. **Firm-level vs project-specific separation explicit.** §4 contains the firm-level recommendations that should change Northwood standard practice (10 recommendations). §5 contains the project-specific lessons that should not (4 lessons). The separation prevents firm-level adoption of patterns that are not transferable. The Week 0 framework's pre-classification flags have been resolved at closeout: items pre-flagged [Firm-level candidate] at Week 0 are either confirmed Firm-level (most) or re-classified Project-specific (one — the geotechnical lesson re-classified Firm-level via cross-portfolio Skyhawk evidence).

5. **Adoption status tracked at the lesson level, with explicit closeout-prospective hedging.** Each firm-level recommendation in §4 has a target-adoption-quarter status. The lessons-learned document is a *living artefact* updated through the first year post-closeout as adoption decisions land.
