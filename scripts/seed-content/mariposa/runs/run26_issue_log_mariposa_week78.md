**I-029 — Performance-test blade-pitch calibration drift on 2 of 80 turbines (Week 73, closed Week 75).** *Discovery context:* During the 168-hour OEM availability test conducted across the fleet at performance testing, 78 of 80 turbines passed first attempt. Turbines at positions 47 and 62 failed the availability test on first attempt due to blade-pitch calibration drift detected during the extended test cycle. The drift mode is distinct from the FAT lead-unit drift (I-024 Week 32): I-024 was a manufacturing-side calibration drift detectable at FAT; I-029 is a wear-in calibration drift visible only under extended operational test, characteristic of normal commissioning-phase tuning rather than design or manufacturing fault. *Engineering response:* OEM mobilised re-calibration team Week 73; both turbines re-calibrated and re-tested under the OEM PO warranty terms; both passed availability test on second attempt within the same week. The 78 of 80 first-pass rate (97.5%) is within OEM-stated fleet-level expectation and within Northwood's bid-assumption for performance-test pass rate. *Commercial resolution:* No Northwood cost — re-calibration and re-test performed under OEM warranty obligation in the PO terms negotiated Week 18 (per I-002 closure). Performance bond mechanism not invoked; standard warranty workflow handled the event. *Linkage:* R-001 (single-OEM concentration) and R-006 (OEM design-interface) further tested through this event; both confirmed mitigated — the drift was operational tuning, not design failure, and the OEM warranty mechanism functioned correctly. *Lesson learned:* Fleet-level performance testing on 80-unit single-OEM projects should budget for ~2–3% first-pass failure on the 168-hour availability test, with second-attempt resolution under OEM warranty as the standard pattern — not as an exception. This refines the I-024 FAT-discipline lesson by extending the "expect findings, time-budget them" principle from manufacturing acceptance to operational acceptance. Recommend Northwood standard for single-OEM fleet projects above 50 units: explicit performance-test contingency window of 7–10 working days at the fleet-level availability test stage.

## 4. Issue categorisation conventions

Eight categories are used. Each issue belongs to exactly one primary category; secondary categorisation is permitted via slash notation where the issue genuinely spans two (e.g., I-029 Technical primary with no secondary — although the event has commercial-relevant warranty linkage, the substance of the issue is technical and the commercial channel is the standing PO warranty mechanism, not a new commercial event).

- **Technical** — engineering, design, or technology issues.
- **Schedule** — issues affecting milestone dates, sequence, or float.
- **Cost** — issues affecting budget, cost-to-complete, or contingency consumption (internal to Northwood; distinct from Commercial).
- **Commercial** — issues affecting the contract — change orders, variations, claims, payment timing.
- **Regulatory** — issues with regulators, permits, compliance — federal, state, local.
- **Safety** — issues affecting site safety, OSHA reporting, near-misses, lost-time events.
- **Quality** — issues affecting quality of delivered work, ITP failures, non-conformances, punch items.
- **Stakeholder** — issues with people: assignments pending, client interface friction, subcontractor performance below expectation.

*Construction-category note (retained from Week 52):* Three issues (I-025, I-027, and informally I-031 walkdown finding) carry "Construction" or "Quality" as working tags at the operational reporting layer used by the Construction Manager and QA/QC Lead; for category-discipline purposes these are classified Technical or Quality primary in the audit register per the eight-category taxonomy.

Severity is graded L/M/H per Northwood standard. H-severity issues require immediate PM-Director escalation per communications plan §4; M-severity issues are tracked weekly in the internal status meeting; L-severity issues are owner-resolved without escalation. At Week 78, no issue is Open; all seven H-severity issues opened across the project (I-001, I-002, I-003, I-004, I-005, I-021, I-026) closed without escalation beyond protocol. Three M-severity issues opened in commissioning and SC closeout (I-029) and prior phases (I-024, I-028) closed within owner-led workstreams.

## 5. Lifecycle states

Issues progress through four states. Transitions are explicit and logged.

- **Open** — issue identified, owner assigned, no resolution path defined yet.
- **In progress** — owner is actively working on resolution; resolution path defined.
- **Resolved** — corrective action complete; awaiting verification.
- **Closed** — resolution verified; issue removed from active register but remains in log for audit.

Issues remain in the log permanently — closure does not delete. The log is append-only with state transitions; this is the discipline that makes the log auditable at closeout and useful as a lessons-learned input. At Week 78, all 31 issues are Closed. The clean closeout state is the appropriate end-of-execution position; any new issues during the warranty tail (Weeks 79–82 grid energisation, then 24-month warranty period) will be opened in the post-SC warranty log per the warranty workflow, not appended to this construction-phase log.

## 7. Conventions used

Five patterns this document follows, for traceability against the Northwood worked-example convention:

1. **Issues are things that happened; risks are things that may happen.** The risk register is forward-looking; this issue log is backward-and-current looking. At closeout the discipline is fully exercised: every Realised risk has at least one Linked-issue audit trail (R-007/I-026, R-009/I-021, R-010/I-028); every Mitigated risk has issue-driven evidence of mitigation in execution; the single Not-Materialised risk (R-012) carries no linked issue, which is the empirical record of non-materialisation.
2. **Categorisation is single-primary with optional secondary slash.** Each issue has one primary category and at most one slash-noted secondary. Seven of the 31 issues at Week 78 use secondary categorisation; the discipline holds across the expanded log without drift.
3. **Severity drives escalation behaviour, not just labelling.** The seven H-severity issues opened across the project all triggered the appropriate escalation: PM-Director on opening for in-execution H items (I-021, I-026), Sponsor brief on threshold crossing (I-028 via R-010 protocol — M-severity but threshold-escalated). I-029 was tracked at the weekly internal status meeting per M-severity protocol and did not require Director escalation given OEM-warranty resolution mechanism.
4. **Dictionary entries only for material issues.** Nine dictionary entries across the project: I-002, I-003, I-004, I-005, I-021, I-024, I-026, I-028 (Iterations 1–2), plus I-029 (Iteration 3). I-030 and I-031 are L-severity items absorbed within branch tolerances and carry log entries only. I-001 remains omitted from the dictionary (closed favourably with no commercial event and no portfolio lesson).
5. **The log is append-only; closure does not delete.** All 31 closed issues remain in the log with closure narrative. The log is now the construction-phase audit record; the warranty-tail log will be a separate downstream artefact.
