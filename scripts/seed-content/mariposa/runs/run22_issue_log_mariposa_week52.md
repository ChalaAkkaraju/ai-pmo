**I-021 — Sub-grade soil-bearing variance at Plot B positions 16, 23, 41 (Week 27, closed Week 31).** *Discovery context:* During foundation excavation in Plot B north-west quadrant, geotechnical readings at three positions indicated bearing capacity below original design assumption. *Engineering response:* Extended geotechnical investigation across remaining Plot B positions Weeks 29-31 found no further variance beyond the three confirmed positions; remedial piling and structural fill complete Week 30; foundations 100% complete Week 38 on schedule. *Commercial and schedule resolution:* Total remediation cost $0.18M absorbed within R5 reserve (per budget §4); 5-day foundations milestone slip absorbed within Chain D float; 30-day SC-to-energisation buffer intact. *Linkage:* R-009 (variable-terrain sub-grade variability, portfolio Pattern 4 candidate) was registered Week 27; forward-looking element of R-009 closed Week 31 with no extension. Iteration-1 provisional lesson now confirmed and ready for portfolio elevation at Iteration 3.

**I-024 — FAT findings on lead OEM unit (Week 32, closed Week 33).** *Discovery context:* During Factory Acceptance Testing on the lead turbine unit, two findings surfaced: (a) the OEM-side rotor-hub torque procedure required refinement to meet the OEM's internal QA standard; (b) blade-pitch calibration on the lead unit had drifted beyond OEM tolerance. The lead-unit FAT is the first physical test of the single-OEM concentration risk (charter R1) and the design-interface risk (R-006). *Engineering response:* OEM remediated both findings within FAT timeline; third FAT attempt cleared the lead unit. Refined rotor-hub torque procedure adopted fleet-wide. One OEM-side procedural punch-list item (rotor-hub torque validation) was carried into first-turbine site-erection for site-side validation (subsequently validated at I-027). *Commercial resolution:* No commercial event; FAT findings were OEM-side remediation under PO terms (performance bond mechanism not invoked). *Linkage:* R-006 partially tested via this issue and confirmed mitigated at first-turbine erection (I-027). *Lesson learned:* FAT discipline on a lead unit before fleet manufacture is the right pattern for single-OEM concentration projects; this validates the Northwood FAT requirement in the PO terms. For projects with 80-unit fleets, FAT findings on lead unit should be assumed likely and time-budgeted into the OEM cadence rather than treated as exceptions.

**I-026 — Client request for SCADA portfolio integration (Week 34, closed Week 40, CO-001 executed Week 40).** *Discovery context:* The client requested SCADA portfolio integration with their operations centre (OPCO) — outside the original WBS 8.3 scope which had assumed Mariposa-only SCADA-to-plant-control integration. Future bids on wind erection should retain the R5-pooled approach but should ensure the R5 allocation is sized against the expected number of multi-day weather windows in the erection season's climate band, not against historical project averages.

## 4. Issue categorisation conventions

Eight categories are used. Each issue belongs to exactly one primary category; secondary categorisation is permitted via slash notation where the issue genuinely spans two (e.g., I-026 "Commercial" with linkage to Technical SCADA scope; I-028 "Schedule / Weather" where weather is the operational sub-category of Schedule for wind-erection-specific tracking).

- **Technical** — engineering, design, or technology issues.
- **Schedule** — issues affecting milestone dates, sequence, or float.
- **Cost** — issues affecting budget, cost-to-complete, or contingency consumption (internal to Northwood; distinct from Commercial).
- **Commercial** — issues affecting the contract — change orders, variations, claims, payment timing.
- **Regulatory** — issues with regulators, permits, compliance — federal, state, local.
- **Safety** — issues affecting site safety, OSHA reporting, near-misses, lost-time events.
- **Quality** — issues affecting quality of delivered work, ITP failures, non-conformances, punch items.
- **Stakeholder** — issues with people: assignments pending, client interface friction, subcontractor performance below expectation.

*Construction-category note:* Two Iteration-2 issues (I-025, I-027) carry "Construction" as a working sub-tag of Technical at the operational reporting layer used by the Construction Manager; for category-discipline purposes these are classified Technical primary in the audit register.

Severity is graded L/M/H per Northwood standard. H-severity issues require immediate PM-Director escalation per communications plan §4; M-severity issues are tracked weekly in the internal status meeting; L-severity issues are owner-resolved without escalation. At Week 52, no issue is Open; all H-severity issues opened during the project (I-001, I-002, I-003, I-004, I-005, I-021, I-026) are closed.

## 5. Lifecycle states

Issues progress through four states. Transitions are explicit and logged.

- **Open** — issue identified, owner assigned, no resolution path defined yet.
- **In progress** — owner is actively working on resolution; resolution path defined.
- **Resolved** — corrective action complete; awaiting verification.
- **Closed** — resolution verified; issue removed from active register but remains in log for audit.

Issues remain in the log permanently — closure does not delete. The log is append-only with state transitions; this is the discipline that makes the log auditable at closeout and useful as a lessons-learned input. At Week 52, all 28 issues are Closed. This clean state is appropriate for the mid-erection lifecycle position with foundations complete, OEM manufacturing complete, and erection in steady-state operation; further issues are expected to surface during the remaining 28 turbine erections (Weeks 53-65) and commissioning (Weeks 65 onward).
