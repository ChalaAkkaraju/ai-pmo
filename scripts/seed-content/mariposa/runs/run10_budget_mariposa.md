## 4. Contingency allocation reasoning

The $5.5M contingency is held by the PM as a discretionary fund. It is not pre-allocated to specific WBS branches at the baseline date, but the analytical allocation below shows the PM's reasoning at planning, mapped to the five charter §8 risks:

| Risk source | Allocation ($k) | Tied to |
|---|---:|---|
| R1 — Single-OEM turbine supply concentration (80 units) | 1,800 | Largest concentration risk on the project. Covers expediting, performance-bond claim preparation, and partial cost of re-qualification if a tranche fails FAT. Performance bond is the primary recourse; contingency is the residual exposure. |
| R2 — Federal tax-credit deadline (30 Dec year+1) | 1,200 | Schedule-cost crossover: acceleration cost (overtime, additional crews, expedited shipping) if the 30-day SC-to-energisation buffer is threatened. The deadline itself is hard; the cost channel is recovery spend. |
| R3 — Migratory bird flyway blackout periods | 700 | Sequence re-engineering cost if blackout windows compress productive construction season; potential additional crews during productive windows. Magnitude depends on blackout window confirmation. |
| R4 — Rural-road upgrades (two counties) | 800 | Two-county DOT coordination risk; covers scope growth on either county's upgrade if condition surveys reveal more extensive work than scoped, or DOT-imposed restoration obligations. |
| R5 — Rural labour market / camp accommodation | 500 | Retention bonuses, partner recruitment cost, camp cost variance against build-vs-lease decision. |
| Unallocated reserve | 500 | PM discretionary; held for unanticipated discoveries (geotechnical, weather extremes, OEM design-interface surprises) |
| **Total contingency** | **5,500** | Charter §7 confirmed |

Of the $5.5M, $5.0M maps to the five charter §8 risks (R1–R5) and $0.5M is held as unallocated reserve. The PM may re-allocate within the envelope as risks materialise or close. Expansion beyond $5.5M requires Director approval per charter §11 governance ($1M ceiling on Director authority; anything beyond that goes to Sponsor).

The heavy weight on R1 ($1.8M, 33% of contingency) reflects the single-OEM concentration across 80 units — the largest single-OEM PO Northwood has executed. The combined R1 + R2 allocation ($3.0M, 55% of contingency) reflects that the OEM cadence and the tax-credit deadline are the two interlocked risks that dominate the project's commercial outcome.

## 6. Cost reporting framework

### 6.1 Variance reporting cadence

- **Weekly internal:** cost-to-complete forecast vs P50 baseline, by WBS branch. Variance >2% on any branch flagged for review.
- **Monthly client status:** invoiced-vs-baseline at milestone level; no branch-level disclosure to client.
- **Monthly portfolio:** cumulative cost variance, contingency consumed-to-date, contingency-remaining vs. risks-remaining.

### 6.2 Earned value method

The project uses cost-plus-progress earned-value calculations:

- BCWP (Budgeted Cost of Work Performed) at each branch, calculated from progress percentages reported by Engineering, Procurement, and Construction leads.
- ACWP (Actual Cost of Work Performed) from invoiced and accrued cost data.
- BCWS (Budgeted Cost of Work Scheduled) from the schedule baseline.
- CPI (Cost Performance Index) = BCWP / ACWP. Target ≥ 1.00. CPI < 0.95 on any branch triggers PM-Director conversation; CPI < 0.90 triggers re-baseline review.

### 6.3 Re-baseline thresholds

- Single-branch overrun exceeding $400k OR exceeding 10% of branch P50, whichever is smaller: cost update, no re-baseline.
- Single-branch overrun exceeding $1.0M OR exceeding 20% of branch P50: re-baseline that branch with rationale; change-order if client-driven, internal-only if Northwood-driven.
- Total project cost overrun exceeding $3.3M (60% of contingency) OR margin projection dropping below 5.7% (full-contingency-consumed floor): portfolio escalation per charter §11 governance; immediate Sponsor brief.

## 8. Conventions used

Five patterns this document follows, for traceability against the Northwood worked-example convention:

1. **Cost baseline as P50 plus separately-tracked contingency.** The P50 budget ($134.0M) is the operational target. The $5.5M contingency is tracked separately. Combining them obscures both the operating-cost discipline and the risk-driven contingency reasoning.
2. **Decomposition by WBS branch with confidence bands.** Each Level-2 WBS branch carries a P50 cost and a confidence band (Tight / Medium). At Week 0 baseline, six branches carry Medium bands — these drive the contingency allocation. Tightening is expected through Engineering 30% and PO/subcontract award.
3. **Contingency allocation reasoned against the risk register.** The $5.5M contingency is mapped to the five charter §8 risks (R1–R5) plus a $0.5M unallocated reserve. Heavy weight on R1 + R2 (55% combined) reflects the dominant commercial-outcome risks: single-OEM concentration and the hard tax-credit deadline.
4. **Cash flow tied to milestone schedule with net working capital tracked at each milestone.** The milestone-by-milestone cash projection uses the Schedule Reasoner's milestone table as the reference. Many dates carry forward as flagged from the schedule baseline at this Week 0 stage.
5. **Re-baseline thresholds in dollars and percentages, not judgement calls.** Single-branch and total-project thresholds are explicit. The total-project threshold ($3.3M = 60% of contingency, and 5.7% margin floor) is the trigger for Sponsor escalation per charter §11.

## 9. Notes for downstream agents

- **Status Reporter.** Cost-variance reporting uses the WBS-branch table in §3 as the reference. The CPI ≥ 1.00 target and the 5.7% margin floor are the operational metrics to track week-on-week. Branch 3.0 Turbine OEM dominates the cost base at 50%; any movement on this branch is material to project commercial outcome. Any branch reaching CPI < 0.95, or any contingency consumption that leaves an R-tied risk uncovered, is a status-report material item.
- **Risk Analyst.** The five cost-linked risks in §7 are a filtered view of the charter §8 register. R1 (OEM concentration) is the highest-impact cost-channel risk; the $1.8M allocation reflects the single-OEM concentration across 80 units. R2 (tax-credit deadline) is cost-linked through acceleration spend rather than direct cost — if the SC-to-energisation buffer erodes, the cost channel opens. If new risks emerge during execution with cost impact (OEM design interface at WBS 2.11, two-county DOT interfaces at WBS 4.2/4.3 are the likely candidates), the contingency-allocation analysis in §4 updates with them.
- **Variance Analyst.** This document is the cost baseline against which variance is measured. Re-baseline events per §6.3 update this document; the change log at the head of the WBS dictionary records each event with date and rationale. The Week 0 confidence bands are expected to tighten through Engineering 30%, OEM PO acceptance, and subcontract award — variance reporting should distinguish band-tightening updates from genuine cost movement.
- **Change Order Reviewer.** Client-driven scope changes update the contract value (charter §7), the affected WBS branches (§3), and the cost-to-complete projection (§5 milestone table). The change-order analysis package always includes the marginal impact on the margin floor. Note: client-driven changes affecting energisation date or permitted construction methods require client approval per charter §11 — these are commercial events first, scope events second.
