## 3. Issue dictionary — significant issues

Dictionary entries are provided here for the H-severity issues. Each represents a material open item where commitment is currently absent and where failure to close before the relevant downstream trigger date would propagate as schedule, cost, or commercial loss.

**I-001 — Client Lead name and engagement preferences confirmation (Week 0).** *Discovery context:* The charter §5 stakeholder table flags the primary client interface as `[NEEDS PM REVIEW: client lead name]`. The communications plan §2 and §3.1 commit Northwood to monthly written status, weekly informal voice calls, biweekly site walks post-mobilisation, and immediate escalation on energisation-impacting events — all of which require a named, engaged counterpart on the client side from Week 1. *Operational response:* PM to make formal request to Mariposa Renewables Holdings within Week 1; client lead identification expected within Week 2. *Commercial resolution:* No direct commercial event, but absence of a confirmed client interface degrades every escalation pathway in communications plan §4. *Lesson learned (provisional):* For tax-credit-driven projects where the energisation date is the central commercial commitment, client lead confirmation should be a contract-execution precondition, not a Week 0 open item.

**I-002 — Long-lead turbine OEM PO release date (Week 0).** *Discovery context:* Charter §9 Assumption 4 flags the long-lead turbine PO release date as an open intake item. WBS 3.1 dictionary identifies this as the largest single procurement on the project (50% of cost base per budget §3). The schedule baseline (run09 §3 Chain B) is gated by 60% Engineering, and the OEM cadence drives every downstream chain through to energisation. *Operational response:* PM and Procurement Lead to confirm PO release window against 60% Engineering completion target; OEM organisation and account lead to be named in parallel (see I-007). *Commercial resolution:* PO terms must include contracted delivery cadence, performance bond, escalation rights, and FAT on lead units (WBS 3.11) per charter §8 R1 mitigation. *Linkage:* Charter risk R1 is the forward-looking risk; this issue is the present-state open item that gates whether R1 mitigation actually takes effect. *Lesson learned (provisional):* Single-OEM concentration risks of this scale require PO-readiness tracking from the day of contract signature, not from the day of OEM selection.

**I-003 — Migratory bird permit blackout windows (Week 0).** *Discovery context:* Charter §10 Constraint 3 declares migratory bird flyway permit blackout periods non-negotiable. Charter §9 Assumption 3 assumes blackout windows are consistent with the bid schedule but flags them as pending client confirmation. Schedule baseline (run09 §3 Chain D) treats site clearing and grading sequencing (WBS 5.2) as gated by these windows. *Operational response:* PM with QA/QC Lead to obtain written confirmation from client of blackout dates within Week 3; if blackouts differ materially from bid assumptions, schedule Chain D resequencing required and contingency drawdown against the $700k R3 allocation triggered. *Commercial resolution:* Blackout windows are regulatory facts, not negotiable scope; the cost channel is sequence re-engineering and additional crews during productive windows. *Linkage:* Charter risk R3 is the forward-looking risk; this issue is the present-state confirmation gap. *Lesson learned (provisional):* For projects in protected-species flyways, written confirmation of blackout windows should be a Week 0 deliverable from the client, not an early-execution-phase open item.

**I-004 — Camp accommodation build-vs-lease decision (Week 0).** *Discovery context:* Charter §9 Assumption 6 flags camp accommodation as unresolved. WBS 1.8 and 3.12 carry the operational and procurement sides of the decision. Budget §3 shows the camp line at $1.7M with a Medium confidence band, noting material capex-vs-opex variance against the arrangement chosen. The decision is required before site mobilisation. *Operational response:* PM to complete build-vs-lease analysis within Week 6; rural labour market assessment to inform the choice; decision documented and submitted for Director approval if it exceeds PM authority. *Commercial resolution:* Decision affects budget structure (capex vs operating cost), schedule mobilisation, and productivity throughout construction; contingency allocation of $500k against R5 is the residual exposure on the productivity channel. *Linkage:* Charter risk R5 is the forward-looking risk; this issue is the present-state decision gap. *Lesson learned (provisional):* Rural-location project bids should price the camp build-vs-lease decision before contract signature where labour-market intelligence permits, not carry it as an open assumption into execution.

**I-005 — Interconnection study status and utility coordinator name (Week 0).** *Discovery context:* Charter §9 Assumption 2 flags the interconnection study as an open intake item; the assumption is that the study completes in time to support 30 December year+1 energisation. Charter §5 leaves the utility coordinator and the regional utility unnamed. Schedule baseline (run09 §2) identifies grid interconnection energisation as the hard tax-credit-deadline milestone and the single most important commitment on the project. *Operational response:* PM to confirm utility coordinator name and current interconnection study status within Week 2; monthly utility coordination call to be established per communications plan §3.2 from contract effective date through energisation. *Commercial resolution:* If the interconnection study is materially delayed, the SC-to-energisation buffer (30 days) is the only float, and any erosion triggers Sponsor escalation per communications plan §4. The cost channel beyond the buffer is LDs to $11.84M cap and tax-credit-tier loss exposure. *Linkage:* Charter risk R2 is the forward-looking risk; this issue is the present-state visibility gap on the central deliverable. *Lesson learned (provisional):* For tax-credit-deadline-driven projects, interconnection study status should be confirmed at proposal stage, not at Week 0 of execution.

## 4. Issue categorisation conventions

Eight categories are used. Each issue belongs to exactly one primary category; secondary categorisation is permitted via slash notation (e.g., "Schedule / Commercial" for I-002, which is both an OEM PO release-date issue and a commercial-terms-of-procurement issue).

- **Technical** — engineering, design, or technology issues.
- **Schedule** — issues affecting milestone dates, sequence, or float.
- **Cost** — issues affecting budget, cost-to-complete, or contingency consumption (internal to Northwood; distinct from Commercial).
- **Commercial** — issues affecting the contract — change orders, variations, claims, payment timing.
- **Regulatory** — issues with regulators, permits, compliance — federal, state, local.
- **Safety** — issues affecting site safety, OSHA reporting, near-misses, lost-time events.
- **Quality** — issues affecting quality of delivered work, ITP failures, non-conformances, punch items.
- **Stakeholder** — issues with people: assignments pending, client interface friction, subcontractor performance below expectation.

Severity is graded L/M/H per Northwood standard. H-severity issues require immediate PM-Director escalation per communications plan §4; M-severity issues are tracked weekly in the internal status meeting; L-severity issues are owner-resolved without escalation. At Week 0 baseline, five H-severity issues are flagged — all derived from charter open items that gate central commercial commitments.

## 5. Lifecycle states

Issues progress through four states. Transitions are explicit and logged.

- **Open** — issue identified, owner assigned, no resolution path defined yet.
- **In progress** — owner is actively working on resolution; resolution path defined.
- **Resolved** — corrective action complete; awaiting verification.
- **Closed** — resolution verified; issue removed from active register but remains in log for audit.

Issues remain in the log permanently — closure does not delete. The log is append-only with state transitions; this is the discipline that makes the log auditable at closeout and useful as a lessons-learned input. At Week 0 baseline, all 20 issues are in Open state; transition to In progress is expected within Weeks 1–4 as owners commit resolution paths.

## 7. Conventions used

Five patterns this document follows, for traceability against the Northwood worked-example convention:

1. **Issues are things that happened; risks are things that may happen.** The charter §8 risk register is forward-looking; this issue log is backward-and-current looking. At Week 0 baseline, the issues are present-state open items derived from upstream artefacts, not hypothetical operational events. Risks (R1–R5 per charter §8) cross-reference here only where their associated open items are present-state.
2. **Categorisation is single-primary with optional secondary slash.** Eight categories, each issue gets one primary category, optional second category after a slash where the issue genuinely spans two. Multi-categorisation discipline prevents the log from becoming an unstructured grab-bag.
3. **Severity (L/M/H) drives escalation behaviour, not just labelling.** H-severity triggers immediate PM-Director escalation per communications plan §4; M-severity triggers weekly attention; L-severity is owner-resolved. The grade carries operational meaning.
4. **Dictionary entries only for material issues.** Five dictionary entries at Week 0 — one per H-severity issue. M-severity and L-severity issues carry log entries without dictionary paragraphs unless they escalate or surface lessons-learned material.
5. **The log is append-only; closure does not delete.** Closed issues remain in the log permanently. This is the discipline that makes the log auditable at closeout and useful as a lessons-learned input.
