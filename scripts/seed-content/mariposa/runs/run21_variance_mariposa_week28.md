## 6. Variance root cause analysis

One material variance event at Week 28 crosses the materiality threshold (>$0.2M cost OR >7 days schedule). The sub-grade event at I-021 is at $0.18M cost (just below cost threshold) and 5 days schedule (below threshold). It is analysed here because of its forward-risk implications via R-009 and its linkage to portfolio Pattern 4 candidate, not because the present numbers cross thresholds.

### 6.1 Sub-grade soil-bearing variance at Plot B positions 16, 23, 41 ($0.18M cost, 5 days schedule)

**Trigger event.** During foundation excavation at three turbine positions in the Plot B north-west quadrant, civil subcontractor flagged soft soil conditions at position 16 in Week 27. Geotechnical investigation extended across positions 23 and 41 same week confirmed bearing capacity below the original design assumption at all three positions. Pattern is consistent across the three positions in a localised Plot B sub-grade variability footprint.

**Decision sequence.** Week 27: civil subcontractor flagged soft soil at position 16; PM ordered extended geotechnical investigation across Plot B north-west quadrant; results confirmed broader sub-grade weakness in three positions. Week 28: revised foundation design with remedial piling and additional structural fill approved by Engineering Lead; civil subcontractor mobilising additional equipment for execution Weeks 29–30. H-severity issue I-021 opened Week 27 and escalated to Project Director per protocol.

**Root cause classification.** **External — site-conditions variance** (natural/site sub-category per the Northwood six-category scheme). Geotechnical investigation at proposal stage followed standard density for variable-terrain wind sites; the affected Plot B north-west quadrant did not feature in the original sampling grid which was concentrated in higher-risk topographic positions. The same pattern appeared on Skyhawk Plot B sub-grade variance per portfolio review §4. *(at draft stage [NEEDS PM REVIEW: classification confirmed; cross-reference to portfolio Pattern 4 candidate is established via R-009 in the Week 28 risk register])*

**Lesson learned (provisional).** For variable-terrain greenfield wind sites, geotechnical investigation grid density at proposal stage may be insufficient where sub-grade variability is plausible. Updated bid-stage standard for similar projects should specify enhanced geotechnical scope at proposal pricing, distinct from general contingency. This is the second realisation of the pattern in the Northwood active portfolio (Skyhawk being the first); the Lessons-Learned Synthesiser and Portfolio Risk Reviewer at Iteration 2 (Week 52) will determine whether this is sufficient evidence to elevate Pattern 4 candidate to confirmed pattern.

### 6.2 Sub-material favourable variances retained for transparency

Two favourable variances did not cross materiality thresholds individually for full root-cause analysis but are noted for the picture they form.

- **OEM PO favourable ($0.60M, I-002 closed Week 18).** Cause: OEM pricing discipline and Northwood's contracted-cadence negotiation. Classification: External — vendor (favourable). Lesson: single-OEM PO-readiness tracking from contract signature works; the discipline of treating PO-readiness as a present-state issue closed 6 weeks before the schedule baseline's 60% gate with favourable cost outcome.
- **Camp lease favourable ($0.15M, I-004 closed Week 6).** Cause: lease arrangement at lower cost than bid build-vs-lease analysis assumed. Classification: Methodological — bid-stage estimating treated camp decision as undifferentiated allowance; market-tested lease came in below allowance. Lesson: for rural-location projects where labour-market intelligence is available pre-contract, the build-vs-lease decision can be priced in the bid.
