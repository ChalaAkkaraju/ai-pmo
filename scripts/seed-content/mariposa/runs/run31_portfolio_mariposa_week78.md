## 3. Cross-cutting risk taxonomy

Northwood's portfolio risk review uses six cross-cutting risk classes. The taxonomy is closed; classification outside the six is either project-specific or warrants a PMO Director decision on extending the taxonomy.

- **Vendor / supplier concentration.** Single-vendor or single-supplier dependencies where performance failure cascades to project outcome. Portfolio-relevant when multiple projects depend on overlapping vendor pools or when vendor-class behaviour recurs across projects.
- **Regulatory / external deadline.** Regulator-imposed schedule constraints — permit expiry, standard effective dates, tax-credit deadlines, court-ordered consent decrees, utility-driven energisation windows. Portfolio-relevant when regulator class appears across multiple projects or when deadline-driven pressure is a consistent pattern.
- **Site-conditions variance.** Site-specific discoveries during construction — geotechnical surprises, latent defects, environmental contamination, hidden infrastructure. Portfolio-relevant when project class (brownfield, variable-terrain greenfield, urban) recurs.
- **Resource / labour scarcity.** Local labour market, skilled-trade availability, accommodation, transport access. Portfolio-relevant when project class (rural location, regional construction boom, specialised-trade-dependent) recurs.
- **Client-driven scope or sequence changes.** Client-initiated mid-execution changes. Portfolio-relevant when contract structure (fixed-price vs T&M) interacts with client class (IPP, municipal, industrial owner-operator) in patterns that erode margin or compress schedule.
- **Weather / climate-sensitive construction.** Seasonal, climatic, or weather-event windows. Portfolio-relevant when project class (outdoor renewables, marine, indoor industrial) and geography produce recurring patterns.

The taxonomy is closed. The Iteration-2 candidate (tax-credit-deadline pre-contract confirmation discipline gap) is now resolved against Mariposa execution evidence: the five Week 0 H-severity issues all closed on plan without escalation breaching protocol (I-001 Week 2, I-002 Week 18, I-003 Week 5, I-004 Week 6, I-005 Week 38), suggesting the discipline-gap concern was a Mariposa-specific Week 0 baseline state rather than a recurring portfolio pattern. The candidate is folded into the existing Regulatory / external deadline class as a proposal-stage-confirmation-discipline sub-channel; no new closed-taxonomy class warranted at this iteration. *(at draft stage [NEEDS PM REVIEW: PMO Director final decision on whether the resolved-on-Mariposa candidate warrants standalone codification in the taxonomy or sub-channel within Regulatory / external deadline])*.

## 5. Pattern emergence analysis

A pattern is at threshold when two or more projects carry an Active or Realised entry in the same cross-cutting class. Mariposa's closeout state contributes Realised entries to three classes (Site-conditions variance, Client-driven scope changes, Weather) and Mitigated entries to three (Vendor concentration, Regulatory deadline, Resource scarcity); the Mitigated entries also count toward pattern recurrence at portfolio level because the pattern is about the risk class's recurrence, not realisation outcome.

**Pattern 1: Sole-source critical equipment dependency — strengthening continues, firm-level elevation recommended.** Held at threshold from Iteration-1 baseline (Skyhawk BESS active, Riverside blowers realised at 14-week slip, Ironvale furnace OEM mitigated through MC) and reinforced at Iteration 2 by Mariposa's Week 0 single-OEM concentration. At Iteration 3, Mariposa's full execution arc closes as **the most complete renewables-scale validation of post-Ironvale discipline to date**: $0 of $1.8M R1 contingency drawn against the largest single-OEM PO Northwood has executed ($66.4M). The post-Ironvale Q3 Operations Standards update (monthly financial-health monitoring + FAT-on-lead-units + sole-source risk classification + on-site expediter) executed cleanly at 80-unit renewables scale across all four mitigation channels. The I-029 finding (2 of 80 turbines failed availability test on first attempt, closed under OEM warranty Week 75 at zero Northwood cost) refines the standard: wear-in calibration drift detected during the 168-hour fleet availability test is a distinct mode from FAT manufacturing-drift, characteristic of operational acceptance, and warrants an explicit 7-10 working day performance-test contingency window. **Recommend firm-level standard practice elevation for all single-OEM PO above $20M with explicit fleet-level performance-test contingency window on projects above 50 units.**

**Pattern 2: Regulator-imposed external deadlines — confirmed, with renewables-scale buffer-sufficiency evidence.** Held at threshold from Iteration-1 baseline across three regulatory sub-classes (utility interconnection, state environmental, state air quality). Mariposa contributes federal tax-credit deadline class (R-002) plus migratory bird flyway class (R-003) plus utility interconnection class (R-008) plus county DOT class (R-004) — four sub-classes within one project. **Mariposa's 30-day SC-to-energisation buffer survived three material in-construction events without erosion** (I-021 sub-grade absorbed in Chain D float; I-028 weather absorbed in Chain E float; Chain E float consumption Week 52 recovered via pace optimisation Week 60). The buffer-sufficiency-against-mid-construction-shocks lesson is confirmed at closeout — 30 days is the validated minimum for tax-credit-deadline renewables work, not a longer baseline required. *(at draft stage [NEEDS PM REVIEW: confirm pattern carries with Portfolio Members 2, 3, 4; threshold held from prior baseline irrespective of placeholder confirmation])*. **Recommend firm-level Commercial / Proposal Standards codification of the 30-day buffer as the validated minimum.**

**Pattern 3: Client-driven mid-construction scope additions on fixed-price contracts — strengthened with quantitative anchor, firm-level elevation recommended.** Held at threshold from Iteration-1 baseline (Riverside CO-003 UV at 6.7% realised margin pre-discipline; Skyhawk CO-003 SCADA projected). The structurally-predicted pattern produced one correctly-anticipated realisation that was absorbed without margin erosion below the bid floor. **Recommend firm-level Commercial / Contract Standards elevation of the four-frame discipline from "applies to fixed-price strategic-value-client changes" (Iteration-1 standard) to "mandatory standard practice on all client-driven mid-construction scope additions on fixed-price contracts above $250k" with the quantitative anchor cited.**
