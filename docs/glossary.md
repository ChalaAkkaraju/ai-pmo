# Glossary

## EPC, earned-value and financial terms used in this book

**AC — Actual Cost.** The cost actually incurred for the work performed, from the
cost system (SAP PS).

**As-built margin.** Forecast margin at completion: current contract value minus
estimate at completion (EAC).

**As-planned margin.** Margin in the current plan: current contract value minus
current WBS budget.

**As-sold margin.** Margin at booking: sold contract value minus the frozen
baseline budget.

**BAC — Budget at Completion.** The total approved project budget.

**CPI — Cost Performance Index.** EV ÷ AC. Above 1.0 = under cost; below = over.

**CV — Cost Variance.** EV − AC. Negative = over cost.

**Contract asset (WIP).** Revenue recognised in excess of amounts billed — earned
but not yet invoiced.

**Contract liability (deferred revenue).** Amounts billed in excess of revenue
recognised — invoiced ahead of the work earned.

**Contingency.** Budget set aside to cover identified risk; sized against residual
exposure at a P50/P80 confidence level.

**EAC — Estimate at Completion.** The forecast total cost of the project.

**EMV — Expected Monetary Value.** Probability × impact; the risk-weighted cost of
a risk.

**EV — Earned Value.** The budgeted cost of the work actually performed; physical
progress priced at budget.

**Earned schedule.** Schedule performance measured in time units rather than cost,
correcting SPI's end-of-project drift.

**EVM — Earned Value Management.** The standard cost/schedule performance
discipline (ANSI/EIA-748, PMBOK).

**IFRS 15.** The accounting standard governing revenue recognition from customer
contracts.

**Inherent / residual EMV.** Risk-weighted exposure before / after mitigation.

**MTTR — Mean Time To Resolve.** Average time from an issue being raised to closed.

**Peak funding requirement.** The maximum cash a project ties up at once — the
trough of the net cash position.

**POC — Percentage of Completion.** Progress measure; cost-based POC = actual cost
÷ expected cost, used for revenue recognition.

**PV — Planned Value.** The budgeted cost of the work planned to be done by now;
the baseline.

**Recognised revenue.** Revenue that may be booked to date: POC × contract value
(IFRS 15).

**Residual exposure.** Risk-weighted cost remaining after mitigation, for live
risks only.

**Retention.** A percentage of each payment withheld by the client until completion
or end of the defects period.

**SPI — Schedule Performance Index.** EV ÷ PV. Above 1.0 = ahead; below = behind.

**SV — Schedule Variance.** EV − PV. Negative = behind schedule.

**TCPI — To-Complete Performance Index.** The cost efficiency the remaining work
must achieve to hit a target (budget or forecast); the recovery reality test.

**Trend.** A potential change identified before it becomes a funded change order or
an absorbed (unfunded) cost.

**VAC — Variance at Completion.** BAC − EAC; the forecast total over- or under-run.

**WBS — Work Breakdown Structure.** The hierarchical breakdown of project scope;
the code on which cost (SAP) and schedule (P6/MSP) are joined.

**WIP — Work in Progress.** See *Contract asset*.

## IT portfolio terms

**AuC — Assets under Construction.** Capitalised project cost held on the balance
sheet until go-live, then settled to fixed assets; the expense share is posted as
incurred. Cancelling after commit is therefore a write-off decision.

**Authority matrix.** The table that routes each decision — by kind, amount band,
funding source and pre- or post-commit state — to the body that may decide it and
the bodies that must concur first.

**Benefits owner.** The named person who reports realised benefit against the
business case after go-live.

**Bucket.** A slice of the fiscal-year IT envelope for one business technology
(infrastructure, applications, security, compliance). Projects compete inside
their bucket, never across buckets.

**Bucket owner.** The person accountable for one bucket: ranks within it and
approves within delegated limits.

**Change order (IT).** A change to scope, cost or time after the commit gate,
carrying a funding source that decides who approves it.

**Commit gate — Stage Gate 1.** The gate at which scope and budget are locked into
the baseline. Before it a project is exploring; after it every change is a change
order.

**Concurrence.** A required sign-off from a body that does not itself decide —
Finance on the capital / expense split, for example. Approve is locked until every
required concurrence is recorded.

**Continuation.** A running project's request for its next fiscal-year slice,
judged on cost-to-complete against benefit still achievable rather than on the
original business case.

**Decision record.** The workspace's record of one proposal: what was proposed,
who proposed it, the body it was routed to, concurrences, and the decision with
its minutes and attendees.

**Deferred (parked).** A project below the waterline, or displaced by a change
order. It keeps its business case, releases its money, and sits outside the
cumulative until a later waterline.

**Delegation of authority.** The principle that decisions are taken at the lowest
level with the authority for the amount — bucket owner, then CIO, then investment
board.

**Displacement.** A person pulled off a project for a stated reason — incident or
run work, a higher-priority project, an audit demand, a revenue project's
priority — logged with dates and the schedule days attributed.

**Envelope.** The money granted to a project, or to a bucket, for a fiscal year at
the waterline. Granting an envelope does not commit the project.

**Exit criteria.** The named conditions a stage must satisfy at its gate. Go
requires every criterion to be met.

**Fundable amount.** A bucket's allocation less its reserve — the money ranked
against at the waterline.

**Hold.** A gate outcome that pauses the project with a time box; lifted with
Resume by the same authority, or turned into a cancellation.

**Investment board.** The CIO (chair), the Finance controller and the bucket
owners; quorum three. Owns the envelope split, the largest decisions, post-commit
cancellations and displacing change orders.

**Lifecycle state.** Proposed → approved (envelope granted) → active (committed) →
closed, with on hold, deferred and cancelled as side states.

**Mandatory lane.** A bucket (compliance) ranked on cost-to-comply and deadline
rather than on ROI.

**Payback.** Requested budget ÷ annual benefit, in months.

**Ranking score.** ½ × strategic score + ½ × three-year ROI (capped at 300%,
scaled to 0–100) + 2 for hard savings or enablement.

**Reserve.** The part of a bucket's allocation held back from the waterline for
in-year change orders and reserve draws.

**Reserve draw.** An urgent in-year release from a bucket's reserve — CIO up to
the limit, board above — with the board notified afterwards.

**Sanction event.** The auditable record that an amount was authorised: a
waterline envelope, a continuation, a commit baseline.

**Stage-gate template.** The sequence of stages and gates a project follows,
chosen by its category: design & development, deployment / rollout, or
maintenance / upgrade.

**Three-year ROI.** (3 × annual benefit − budget) ÷ budget; the conventional
horizon for comparing IT cases.

**Value type.** What a business case claims: hard savings, soft benefit, risk
reduction, enablement, or compliance / mandatory.

**Waterline.** The line drawn by running the cumulative ranked requests against a
bucket's fundable amount. Above it, funded; below it, deferred. A line, not a
shopping list — nothing below it is picked ahead of the first project that failed
to fit.
